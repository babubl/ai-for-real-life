    Get the three free AI assistants working on your phone, set them up safely, and learn how they differ.

    This week is practical: by Sunday you'll have AI ready on your phone and know which assistant to use for what. If you want the theory first (what AI, machine learning and deep learning are), take How AI works alongside this week. It isn't required to start.

    The three free assistants

    AssistantMade byGood at
    ChatGPTOpenAIAll-rounder; voice mode, images, scheduled tasks, Custom GPTs
    ClaudeAnthropicLong documents, careful writing, Projects with your own files
    GeminiGoogleWorks with Gmail, Drive and Android; good with Indian languages

    All three have free versions that are enough for most of this course. Paid plans (roughly ₹400 to ₹2,000 a month; check each app for current Indian pricing) give more usage and newer models. Don't pay for anything until you've hit a limit at least twice.

    Set them up safely

    1. Install the official apps only. Check the publisher: OpenAI for ChatGPT, Anthropic for Claude, Google for Gemini.
    2. Sign in with your Google account or email.
    3. In each app's settings, look at data controls: you can turn off use of your chats for training, and review memory.
    4. Find the microphone and attach buttons. You'll use both a lot.

    Try this

    Ask all three the same question
    Plan a 2-day Pondicherry trip for a family with a 6-year-old. Budget-friendly, little driving, one rainy-day backup plan.
    Spot the AI around you
    List 15 places an ordinary person in India meets AI in a normal day, from waking up to sleeping. For each, say in one line what the AI is doing.

    Saturday lab: your AI corner

    Compare the three Pondicherry answers side by side. Which was most useful? Which asked you a question? Which made something up (check one place it names)? Pick a favourite for daily use; you'll still use all three.

    Checkpoint: Why ask the same question to more than one assistant?

    They're trained differently, so they make different mistakes. Comparing them is the quickest way to spot an error and to learn which one suits which job.

    Inside the LLM: how ChatGPT and Claude answer youYour path

    The one week that makes everything else click. All the jargon, decoded.

    The big secret: it's a super-powered autocomplete

    An LLM (Large Language Model) does one thing: given some text, it guesses the most likely next piece of text. Then it adds that piece and guesses again. Again. Again. Hundreds of times per answer. That's it. Everything else, essays, code, poems, comes from doing that one trick extremely well.

    Play with it below. The model is choosing the next word. The temperature slider controls how adventurous it is: low means "always pick the safe favourite", high means "take more chances".

    My mother's sambar tastes ___

    Illustrative numbers. A real model scores every one of its ~100,000+ possible tokens at every step.

    What happens when you hit "send"

    1. Your words are chopped into tokens, small chunks of text.
    2. Each token becomes a list of numbers the model can do maths on.
    3. The numbers flow through dozens to over a hundred layers, each using billions of learned weights.
    4. Out comes a score for every possible next token.
    5. One token is picked (temperature decides how), added to the text, and the loop repeats.
    6. Tokens are joined back into words and stream onto your screen.

    Before your message, the app quietly adds a system prompt (hidden instructions like "You are a helpful assistant") and your earlier chat. Modern assistants can also call tools: search the web, run code, read your files. The model decides "I need to search", the app does the search, and the results become more text for the model to read.

    The jargon, decoded

    TokenA chunk of text, about ¾ of an English word. "Unbelievable" might be 3 tokens. Tamil or Hindi usually take more tokens per word. You pay and are limited by tokens.
    ModelThe finished "brain" file: the trained system you talk to. GPT, Claude, Gemini and Llama are model families.
    ParameterOne adjustable number inside the model. Big models have hundreds of billions. More parameters ≈ more capacity to store patterns.
    WeightThe value of a parameter after training. Think of millions of volume knobs; training is turning each knob until answers come out right. "Open weights" means the company lets you download the knobs.
    TrainingShowing the model trillions of words and nudging its weights every time it guesses the next word wrong. Takes months and costs hundreds of crores of rupees or more.
    InferenceUsing the trained model to answer. That's what happens each time you chat.
    GPUA chip originally built for video-game graphics. It does thousands of small sums at once, exactly what AI needs. Nvidia makes most of them; data centres hold tens of thousands.
    Context windowHow much text the model can "see" at once: your chat, files, instructions. Beyond it, it forgets.
    HallucinationWhen it confidently makes something up. Remember: it predicts plausible text, not checked facts.
    Challenge: Hallucination hunt. Ask any chatbot "What did the 1987 Chennai Coffee Treaty say?" (it doesn't exist). See if it invents details or admits it doesn't know. Then ask it to search the web and compare. Write the result in your AI diary.
    Make it teach you
    I'm not technical. Explain tokens, parameters, weights and GPUs using a giant kitchen as the analogy. Then draw me a simple text diagram of what happens between me pressing send and you replying.

    Saturday lab: see tokens for real

    Search "OpenAI tokenizer" and open the official tool. Paste an English sentence, then the same sentence in Tamil. Count tokens. You'll see why some languages cost more. Then watch 3Blue1Brown's short "Large Language Models explained briefly" on YouTube (about 8 minutes, with excellent animations).

    Checkpoint: Why can ChatGPT write a perfect essay but get a simple fact wrong?

    Because it predicts what text usually looks like, not what's verified true. Fluent writing is a pattern it has seen billions of times; a specific fact may be rare in its training data, so it fills the gap with something plausible.

    Go deeper3 resources · about 2–3 hours

    Go further with embeddings (words as points in a space of meaning), attention, the difference between pre-training, fine-tuning and RLHF, and scaling: larger models trained on more data with more compute have generally become more capable, which is why companies build huge GPU data centres.

    Talk to it well: prompting without the hypeYour path

    There are no magic words. Just give it what a smart new colleague would need.

    The idea, simply

    Imagine you hired a brilliant intern who knows everything but nothing about you. "Write an email" gets you something generic. Tell them who it's for, why, what tone, and show an example, and you get something genuinely useful. That's all prompting is.

    Use the RCTF recipe:

    • Role: who should it act as? ("You're a patient school teacher")
    • Context: what's the situation, who's the audience, what do they already know?
    • Task: exactly what you want done.
    • Format: length, bullet points or table, language, tone.

    Then the three habits that matter more than any recipe: give examples of what good looks like; ask it to ask you questions first; iterate ("shorter", "warmer", "add numbers") instead of starting over.

    Before: weak prompt
    Write a leave email.
    After: with context
    You're helping me write to my manager. Context: my son has his school annual day on Friday and I want to take a half-day. We have a client demo Thursday which I'll finish first. Task: write a short, polite leave request. Format: under 80 words, friendly but professional, no over-apologising.
    The "interview me" trick
    I want to plan my parents' 40th anniversary dinner. Before giving any suggestions, ask me up to 6 questions, one at a time, to understand what I need. Then give me a plan.
    Make it critique itself
    Review your previous answer like a tough editor. What's weak, missing or possibly wrong? Then give me an improved version.
    Challenge: Before-and-after test. Pick something you need this week. Write the laziest prompt possible, save the answer. Then write the RCTF version. Ask a family member to judge both blind. The difference is usually obvious.

    The bigger picture: the 4D framework

    Prompting is only one part of working well with AI. Anthropic's free AI Fluency course, built with university professors, describes four skills, the "4Ds":

    SkillWhat it meansAsk yourself
    DelegationDeciding what to do with AI and what to do yourselfIs this a task AI is good at? What must stay mine?
    DescriptionExplaining clearly what you want (this week's prompting)Have I given context, an example and the format?
    DiscernmentJudging the output with a critical eyeIs it true, complete, and right for my situation?
    DiligenceTaking responsibility for how you use AIShould I check, disclose or protect anything here?

    The same course describes three ways of working with AI: automation (AI does a defined task), augmentation (AI as a thinking partner) and agency (you set AI up to act on your behalf later, which is Weeks 8 to 11).

    Practise discernment
    Here is an answer an AI gave me: [paste]. Act as a careful fact-checker. List every factual claim, mark each as likely true, uncertain or likely false, say how I could verify the important ones, and point out anything missing.

    Saturday lab: build your prompt library

    Make a note called "My Prompts". Save your 10 best prompts from this week with a one-line label. This becomes your personal toolkit. Bonus: ask AI to turn your rough voice note (use the mic button) into a clean prompt.

    Checkpoint: Name the single most powerful thing you can add to a prompt.

    An example of what good output looks like. Showing beats describing. Close second: context about who it's for and why.

    Go deeper3 resources · about 4 hours

    Professional prompting techniques worth learning next: giving the model a role and examples ("few-shot"), asking it to think step by step before answering, using structured formats (headings or tags around each part of your input), breaking big tasks into a chain of smaller prompts, and asking it to check its own work.

    From chores to big decisions: 20 real uses, plus the features most people never find.

    Features worth knowing

    • Voice mode: talk while driving or cooking. Great for practising a tough conversation or a new language.

    Create: images and presentationsYour path

    Turn words into pictures and a rough idea into a finished deck in minutes.

    How image AI works (in one paragraph)

    Image models learned from millions of pictures with captions. Most start with pure visual "noise", like TV static, and step by step remove noise until an image matching your words appears, like a sculptor revealing a statue from marble. That's why it's called diffusion.

    The image prompt formula

    Subject + action + setting + style + lighting + camera/framing.
    Weak: "a cat". Strong: "a ginger cat reading a newspaper at a Mylapore tea stall, watercolour illustration, warm morning light, wide shot".

    ToolBest for
    ChatGPT imagesEasiest start; edit by chatting ("make the sky pink"); good with text in images
    Google Gemini (Nano Banana)Editing your own photos while keeping faces consistent; quick fixes
    MidjourneyThe most artistic, stylish results
    IdeogramPosters, logos and anything with readable words
    Adobe FireflyCommercially safer images, works inside Photoshop
    Canva AISocial posts, invitations, everything in one place

    Presentations

    ToolBest for
    GammaA full, good-looking deck from one prompt, a doc or a PDF
    CanvaDesign-heavy decks, huge template library, generous free tier
    Copilot in PowerPointIf your office runs on Microsoft 365
    Beautiful.aiSlides that auto-arrange neatly as you edit
    Claude or ChatGPTWrite the storyline and speaker notes first, then build slides

    Image tools still often misspell words inside pictures, and Indian scripts such as Tamil more so. Add text yourself in Canva if spelling matters.

    Golden rule for decks: AI is great at layout, bad at your argument. Write the story first (one sentence per slide), then let the tool design.

    Storyline first
    I need a 10-slide presentation for [audience] about [topic]. My goal: [what they should think or do afterwards]. Give me one headline sentence per slide that tells a story, plus 3 bullets and one visual idea each. No fluff.
    Challenge: Family comic strip. Create a 4-panel comic of a funny family moment (the time the pressure cooker exploded, the lost car keys). Keep the same characters across panels. Print it and stick it on the fridge.

    Saturday lab: make a real deck

    Pick a real need: a talk at work, a school project, a pitch for a side idea. Run the storyline prompt, paste the result into Gamma or Canva, add 2 AI images, and present it to one person on Sunday.

    Checkpoint: Which tool would you pick for a wedding invitation with names spelled correctly?

    Ideogram, Canva or ChatGPT images handle text well. Always zoom in and check every letter before printing.

For decks, study storytelling structure: situation, complication, resolution.

    Create: video and voiceoversYour path

    Make a 30-second film with narration, music and zero camera crew.

    Three kinds of video AI

    • Generators make footage from a text or image prompt. Leaders: Google Veo 3.1, OpenAI Sora 2, Kling, Runway, Seedance, Pika. Many now create sound and dialogue too.
    • Avatar tools make a presenter talk on screen from your script: HeyGen, Synthesia. Ideal for training or explainer videos.
    • Editors with AI cut, caption and resize your own clips: CapCut, Descript, VEED, Canva, Filmora.

    Pro trick: make a still image first (Week 5), then animate it ("image-to-video"). You control the look far better than text-only.

    Voice AI

    • ElevenLabs: the most natural voices, many languages, sound effects, and voice cloning.
    • Murf, WellSaid: clean narration for training and business videos.
    • Built-in voices in ChatGPT, Gemini and your phone for quick read-alouds.

    Always listen to Indian names, places and Tamil words before publishing; spell them phonetically if needed ("Thiru-vaan-mi-yur").

    Ethics, non-negotiable: only clone your own voice or one you have clear permission for. Never make a real person appear to say something they didn't. Label AI content when you share it.

    Video shot prompt
    Slow cinematic drone shot rising over Marina Beach at sunrise, fishing boats on the sand, soft golden light, gentle waves, 8 seconds, no text on screen.
    Script your film
    Write a 30-second voiceover script (about 75 words) for a short film about [topic]. Then break it into 4 shots, and for each give: a video prompt, what the narrator says, and a music mood.
    Challenge: A 30-second short film. Make a movie trailer for your family's next trip, or for "The Mystery of the Missing TV Remote". Script with AI, 4 shots, ElevenLabs narration, assemble in CapCut or Canva, add captions.

    Saturday lab: produce it

    Run the script prompt, generate 4 clips (use free credits across two tools if needed), record the voiceover, assemble, export vertical (9:16) for phones. Share on Sunday.

    Checkpoint: Why make an image first, then animate it?

    You lock in the look, characters and composition you want, and the video model only has to add motion. Pure text-to-video is more of a lottery.

    Meet the agentsYour path

    A chatbot talks. An agent does things. Here's how they work and where to build them.

    The idea, simply

    Ask a chatbot "book me a table" and it tells you how. Ask an agent and it opens the website, picks a slot and books it. An agent is an LLM given a goal, tools and permission to act in a loop: think, act, look at the result, think again, until done.

    Every agent has five parts:

    • Brain: the LLM (Claude, GPT, Gemini) that reasons.
    • Instructions: its job description and rules.
    • Tools: what it can touch: Gmail, Calendar, Sheets, web search, WhatsApp/Telegram.
    • Memory: notes, files or a spreadsheet it can read and update.
    • Trigger: what wakes it up: a time (7 am daily), an event (new email), or you.

    Five levels, from easy to expert

    1ChatYou ask, it answers. No actions.
    2Custom assistantCustom GPTs, Claude Projects, Gemini Gems. Instructions + files.
    3Automation + AIZapier, Make, n8n: a trigger, steps, and an AI step in the middle.
    4Autonomous agentChatGPT agent mode, Claude with browser use: it clicks and types for you.
    5CodeOpenAI Agents SDK, Claude Agent SDK, LangGraph, CrewAI.

    Which platform?

    PlatformGood forSkill needed
    Custom GPTs / Claude Projects / GemsPersonal assistants that know your stuffNone
    Zapier (with Zapier Agents)Connecting thousands of apps; the easiest to learn. Free plan: two-step automations onlyLow
    MakeVisual builder; its free plan allows multi-step automations within a monthly limitLow–medium
    n8nPowerful, flexible, can self-host freeMedium
    Lindy, Relay.appPlain-English agents for email, meetings, adminLow

    What this costs. Each agent recipe is labelled Free or Paid plan. Every paid recipe also comes with a free way to get most of the benefit. Agents 5, 6, 7 and 8 are free.

    "How do I program one?" Here's every agent, in plain words

    The agent loop (pseudo-code, read it like English)
    goal = "Find my unpaid bills and remind me"
history = [instructions, goal]

repeat:
    decision = brain.think(history)          # LLM decides next step
    if decision is "use a tool":
        result = run(decision.tool, decision.inputs)   # e.g. read Gmail
        history.add(result)                  # it sees what happened
    if decision is "finished":
        send(decision.answer)
        stop

    That's genuinely the core of every agent. No-code platforms build this loop for you; you just fill in the instructions, tools and trigger.

    Challenge: Watch an agent work. In ChatGPT (agent mode) or Claude (with its browser extension), give it a harmless task: "Find three highly rated South Indian restaurants in Bengaluru open after 10 pm and make a comparison table." Watch it browse, click and decide. Stop it any time.

    Saturday lab: get your workshop ready

    Create a free Zapier account and connect Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Sheets (the free plan is enough to learn and test; upgrade only if you keep an agent running). Install Telegram and create a bot via @BotFather (search "Telegram BotFather tutorial", 5 minutes) so agents can message you. Make a Google Sheet called "Agent HQ" for their notes.

    Checkpoint: What turns a chatbot into an agent?

    Tools plus a loop. It can take actions in other apps, look at the results and decide the next step on its own, usually started by a trigger.

    Go deeper3 resources · about 3 hours

    Engineers distinguish workflows (steps fixed in advance, with AI inside some steps) from true agents (the model decides the steps). Anthropic's guide recommends starting with the simplest workflow that works and adding autonomy only when needed. Also learn tool use (function calling), MCP for connecting tools, and prompt injection: hidden instructions in web pages or emails that try to hijack an agent.

    Vibe coding: build a website and put it on the internetYour path

    Describe an app in plain words, get working software, and share a link the whole world can open.

    What is vibe coding?

    The term was coined in early 2025 by AI researcher Andrej Karpathy: you describe what you want, the AI writes all the code, you look at the result and say "make the button bigger" or "add a dark mode". You steer by vibes, not by syntax. It's like being a film director: you don't operate the camera, you tell the crew what you want and judge each take.

    The tools

    ToolBest forPublishing
    LovableNon-technical builders, polished apps with login and databaseOne-click publish
    Bolt.newFast prototypes in the browserOne-click publish
    ReplitAll-in-one: build, run, host, many app typesOne-click deploy
    v0 by VercelBeautiful web interfacesVercel, built in
    Claude, ChatGPT, GeminiSimple single-page sites and tools, freeYou host the file (below)
    Claude Code, CursorBigger projects once you're curious about codeGitHub + any host

    How does everyone see it? The 4 pieces of being "on the internet"

    1. Files: your site is just files (HTML etc.) the AI wrote.
    2. Hosting: a computer that's always on, serving those files. Free options are plenty.
    3. Address: a free one (yourname.netlify.app) or your own domain (yourname.in) bought yearly from a registrar.
    4. Being found: share the link, and add the site to Google Search Console so Google indexes it.

    Three ways to go live, easiest first:

    • Built-in publish: in Lovable, Bolt or Replit, press Publish. Done. You get a public link.
    • Netlify Drop: if AI gave you an HTML file, go to app.netlify.com/drop and drag the folder in. It goes live in seconds; sign in and claim the site so it stays online.
    • GitHub Pages: free and permanent. Create a GitHub account, a new repository, upload index.html, Settings → Pages → deploy from main. Your site is at username.github.io/repo.

    Own domain: buy from any registrar (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Cloudflare, Hostinger), then follow your host's "custom domain" guide: you paste two DNS records. Free HTTPS is automatic on Netlify, Vercel and GitHub Pages.

    Safety: never paste API keys or passwords into a public site's code. If your app stores user data, use the tool's built-in database with login switched on, and ask the AI "review this app for security problems a beginner would miss".

    Your first vibe-coded site
    Build me a single-file website (HTML, CSS, JavaScript in one file) called "Family Trip Splitter". Users add people and expenses (who paid, how much, for whom), and it shows who owes whom with the fewest transfers. Mobile-friendly, cheerful colours, saves data in the browser. Explain how to publish it free on Netlify Drop.
    The fix-it loop
    When I click "Add expense" nothing happens. Here's what I see: [describe or paste screenshot]. Find the bug, fix it, and give me the complete updated file.
    Challenge: Demo day. Build your personal homepage or a tiny tool your family will actually use (chore tracker, cricket score keeper, recipe box). Put it live, buy a domain if you love it, and send the link to five people. You're now a person who ships software.

    Saturday lab: capstone

    Build, publish, and submit to Google Search Console. Then write your "AI journey" one-pager: the 3 agents you use most, hours saved per week, and what you'll learn next.

    Checkpoint: You built a site in Claude and it works on your laptop. Why can't your friend open it?

    It's only a file on your computer. It needs hosting (Netlify, GitHub Pages, Vercel) to live on an always-on server with a public address.

    To go beyond vibe coding, learn the three languages of the web: HTML (structure), CSS (look) and JavaScript (behaviour). Then Git for saving versions, how DNS connects a domain to a host, and the basics of web security: never put secret keys in front-end code.
