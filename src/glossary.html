Glossary

AI words, in plain language.

Every term used on this site, explained in a sentence or two, with a link to where it's taught.

Agent
An AI that doesn't just answer but takes actions toward a goal: it uses tools (email, calendar, web), looks at the results and decides the next step. Week 8
AGI
Artificial General Intelligence: AI that could learn any intellectual task a person can. It doesn't exist yet, and experts disagree on when or whether it will. Lesson 1
Algorithm
A set of step-by-step instructions a computer follows. A learning algorithm is one that improves itself from data.
API
A doorway that lets one program use another, for example your app sending a question to Claude or ChatGPT and getting the answer back.
Artificial intelligence (AI)
Machines doing things that would need intelligence if a person did them: understanding language, recognising images, making decisions. Lesson 1
Attention
The key idea in Transformers: when reading a word, the model weighs every other word to work out which ones matter for its meaning. Lesson 7
Backpropagation
How a neural network learns from a mistake: the error is passed backwards through the layers and every weight is adjusted a tiny amount. Lesson 5
Bias
When AI repeats unfair patterns found in its training data, such as preferring one group of people. Lesson 2
Chatbot
A program you talk to in text or voice. Today's chatbots, such as ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, are powered by large language models.
Classification
Supervised learning that picks a category: spam or not, fraud or genuine. Lesson 3
Clustering
Unsupervised learning that groups similar things together without being told the groups. Lesson 3
Computer vision
The branch of AI that understands images and video: face unlock, Google Lens, defect spotting in factories. Lesson 6
Context window
How much text a model can take in at once: your chat, files and instructions. Beyond it, earlier parts are forgotten. Week 2
Data, dataset
The examples an AI learns from. A dataset is a collection of them, such as 50,000 labelled photos. Lesson 2
Deep learning
Machine learning using neural networks with many layers, which learn complex patterns on their own. Lesson 5
Deepfake
A fake video, photo or voice of a real person made with AI. Safety guide
Diffusion
How most image and video generators work: start from random noise and remove it step by step until a picture matching your words appears. Lesson 7
Epoch
One full pass through the training data. Models are usually trained for many epochs. Lesson 4
Feature
A detail of an example the model looks at, such as a mango's colour or softness. Lesson 2
Fine-tuning
Extra training of an existing model on specialised examples, so it behaves a certain way or knows a certain field. Lesson 7
Generative AI
AI that creates new content (text, images, audio, video) rather than only sorting or judging it. Lesson 7
GPU
A chip first built for video-game graphics that does thousands of small calculations at once, which is exactly what AI needs. Week 2
Hallucination
When AI confidently states something false or made up, because it predicts plausible text rather than checking facts. Week 2
Inference
Using a trained model to produce an answer. Every time you chat, that's inference. Week 2
Label
The correct answer attached to a training example, such as "ripe" or "not ripe". Lesson 2
Large language model (LLM)
A very large neural network trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next token. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are built on LLMs. Week 2
Loss
A number measuring how wrong the model's guesses are. Training tries to make it smaller. Lesson 4
Machine learning (ML)
Teaching computers by showing them examples instead of writing every rule. Lesson 3
MCP
Model Context Protocol: a standard way to connect AI assistants to other apps and data, like a universal plug.
Model
The trained system you interact with: the file of learned weights plus the program that runs it. GPT, Claude, Gemini and Llama are model families. Week 2
Multimodal
A model that handles more than text, such as images, audio and video, in one system. Lesson 6
Natural language processing (NLP)
The branch of AI that understands and produces human language. Lesson 6
Neural network
Layers of simple maths units ("neurons") that each take inputs, weigh them and pass a result on. Lesson 5
Open weights
A model whose trained weights are published so anyone can download and run it, such as Meta's Llama models.
Overfitting
When a model memorises its training examples and does badly on new ones, like a student who memorised last year's answers. Lesson 4
Parameter
One adjustable number inside a model. Large models have hundreds of billions. Week 2
Prompt
What you type or say to an AI. A system prompt is the hidden instruction the app adds before your message. Week 3
RAG
Retrieval-augmented generation: the AI first finds relevant passages in your documents, then answers from them. Reduces made-up answers. Week 11
Regression
Supervised learning that predicts a number, such as a price or a delivery time. Lesson 3
Reinforcement learning
Learning by trial and error with rewards and penalties. Lesson 3
RLHF
Reinforcement learning from human feedback: people rate a model's answers and it's nudged toward the helpful, honest ones. Lesson 7
Supervised learning
Learning from examples that come with the right answers (labels). Lesson 3
Temperature
A setting for how adventurous a model's word choices are: low is predictable, high is creative and riskier. Week 2
Test set
Examples kept aside during training and used only to check how well the model handles data it hasn't seen. Lesson 4
Token
A chunk of text a model reads and writes, roughly three-quarters of an English word. Tamil and Hindi usually use more tokens per word. Week 2
Training
Showing a model many examples and adjusting its weights each time it's wrong. For large models this takes months on thousands of GPUs. Lesson 4
Transformer
The 2017 model design behind every modern LLM, built around attention. Lesson 7
Unsupervised learning
Finding patterns in data that has no labels. Lesson 3
Vibe coding
Building software by describing what you want in plain language and letting AI write the code. Week 12
Weight
The learned value of a parameter: how much one input matters to a neuron. Training is the process of setting weights. Lesson 5
