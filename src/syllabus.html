Learning outcomes

By the end, you will be able to:

Understand

  • Explain what AI, machine learning, deep learning, generative AI and large language models are, and how they relate.
  • Describe how machines solve problems by search, learn from data, handle uncertainty and get evaluated.
  • Explain how ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini produce an answer: tokens, weights, training and next-word prediction.
  • Recognise AI's limits and risks (hallucination, bias, privacy, deepfakes) and the main principles and laws that govern it, including India's DPDP Act.

Apply

  • Use AI assistants well with the 4D framework: decide what to delegate, describe tasks clearly, judge outputs critically and use AI responsibly.
  • Use AI for daily life and work: documents, planning, writing, research, spreadsheets and decisions.
  • Create images, presentations, video and voiceovers with AI tools, ethically.
  • Judge an AI system's results with precision, recall and base rates instead of headline accuracy.

Create

  • Build nine helper agents and assistants that automate real tasks, each with a free option.
  • Scope an AI project using the AI project cycle.
  • Build a website or small app by vibe coding and publish it on the internet with your own domain.

The reference curricula

  • Elements of AI, University of Helsinki. Six chapters: what AI is, problem solving, real-world AI, machine learning, neural networks and implications. Free certificate.
  • CS50's Introduction to AI with Python, Harvard. Search, knowledge, uncertainty, optimisation, learning, neural networks and language. Needs Python.
  • AI for Everyone, Andrew Ng, DeepLearning.AI. What AI is, building AI projects, AI in your company, AI and society.
  • Google AI Essentials. Introduction to AI, productivity, prompting, responsible AI and staying ahead.
  • AI Fluency: Framework and Foundations, Anthropic with university professors. The 4Ds (delegation, description, discernment, diligence) and three modes of working with AI.
  • Microsoft Azure AI Fundamentals (AI-900). AI workloads, machine learning principles, vision, language and responsible AI.
  • CBSE Artificial Intelligence (417), Classes 9–10. The AI project cycle, data literacy, neural networks, computer vision, NLP, model evaluation and Python.
  • UNESCO AI competency framework for students. Twelve competencies across four dimensions (human-centred mindset, ethics, techniques and applications, system design) at three levels: understand, apply, create.

What we cover lightly, on purpose

This site is for everyone, so some university topics are introduced briefly and left to the Go deeper shelves:

  • Formal logic and knowledge representation (CS50 AI lecture 1): important in classic AI, less so for everyday users.
  • The maths of machine learning: gradients, linear algebra and statistics. Pointers are in each lesson's Go deeper shelf.
  • Programming: the course needs none. For Python and building models, see next steps for technical learners.

Three levels of depth

  1. The page itself. Plain-language explanation, examples, an experiment or prompt, and a check question. Enough to understand and use.
  2. Go deeper. Every lesson and week has a shelf with the next ideas to learn and two to four hand-picked free resources, with time estimates.
  3. The full course. Each shelf points to the matching chapter or lecture of a university or official course, so you can take the complete version and, where offered, earn a certificate.
