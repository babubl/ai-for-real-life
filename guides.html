How to choose an AI tool

There are thousands of AI tools and most are wrappers around the same few models. Ask three questions before trying anything new.

  1. Can my everyday assistant already do it? ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini now handle writing, photos, documents, voice, simple images and research. Try there first.
  2. Is it from a company I can check? Look for a real company name, a clear privacy policy and recent updates. Avoid apps that only exist as social media ads.
  3. Is there a free version good enough to test? Use it on a real task for a week. Pay only if you hit the limit twice and it saves you real time.

AI for your shop or small business

You don't need a website to benefit. Most small businesses in India run on WhatsApp, Instagram and Google Maps, so start there.

1. WhatsApp Business catalogue

Write WhatsApp Business catalogue descriptions for these products: [name, size, price for each]. Two short lines each, friendly, mention one benefit, in English and Tamil.

2. Better product photos

Photograph products near a window in daylight. Use Canva's or your phone's background remover for a clean white background, then ask an image tool for a simple festive backdrop. Keep the product itself real; customers should get what they see.

3. Festival offers and posters

Give me 5 ideas for a Pongal offer for my [type of shop] in [area]. Budget-friendly, no big discounts. For the best one, write the poster text and a WhatsApp status message.

Image tools often misspell Tamil words inside pictures. Generate the background with AI, then type the words yourself in Canva.

4. Google Business Profile and reviews

Claim your free listing at google.com/business so you appear on Maps. Use AI to draft replies to reviews, especially unhappy ones:

A customer left this review: [paste]. Write a short, polite reply as the shop owner. Thank them, don't argue, offer to fix it, and invite them to call us.

5. Customer replies and FAQs

Make a Claude Project or Custom GPT with your timings, prices, delivery areas and return policy. Paste customer questions in and get ready replies. Check every price and promise before sending.

6. Simple accounts

Once a month, export your sales or expenses as a spreadsheet, remove bank account numbers, and ask your assistant to total by category and point out unusual entries. Always check against your books; AI can misread numbers. For GST and tax filing, rely on your accountant.

AI in Tamil, Hindi and other Indian languages

You don't need perfect English to use AI. The major assistants understand and reply in Tamil, Hindi, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Bengali, Marathi and more, and they handle mixed language such as Tanglish and Hinglish well.

  • Type or speak in your language. Ask "Reply in Tamil" or "Reply in simple Tamil with English words for technical terms".
  • Voice typing: the microphone on Gboard or your phone keyboard types Tamil and other languages accurately.
  • Google Lens: point your camera at a signboard, menu or form to translate it on the spot.
  • Bhashini (bhashini.gov.in): the Government of India's language technology mission, offering translation and speech tools across Indian languages.
  • Indian AI companies such as Sarvam AI are building models trained specifically for Indian languages.

Know the limits

  • Quality is best in English, then Hindi; for other languages check important text with a fluent speaker.
  • Formal written Tamil and spoken Tamil differ; say which you want.
  • Text inside AI-generated images is often wrong in Indian scripts.
  • AI voices sometimes mispronounce names and places; spell them phonetically.

AI and your job

The honest answer is that nobody knows exactly how AI will change work over the next ten years, and economists disagree. What we can see so far:

  • AI changes tasks more than whole jobs. Drafting, summarising, first-pass analysis, routine replies and simple code are changing fastest.
  • Jobs that mix many kinds of tasks (judgement, relationships, physical work, responsibility for outcomes) change more slowly.
  • Learning AI helps you adapt. People who understand the tools in their own field can take on more, faster.

What to do this month

  1. List your 10 most common work tasks. Mark which ones AI could draft, check or speed up.
  2. Try AI on two of them for two weeks. Keep notes on time saved and mistakes caught.
  3. Learn your employer's AI policy, and never paste confidential data into tools it hasn't approved.
  4. Invest in what AI does poorly: judgement, trust with people, deep knowledge of your domain.

Next steps for technical learners

Finished the course and want to build AI rather than only use it? Here's a free path, roughly in order.

  1. Python basics: Harvard CS50 Python or Kaggle Learn. Both are free.
  2. Maths you'll need: school-level algebra, basic statistics and a little linear algebra. 3Blue1Brown's videos make it visual.
  3. Machine learning: Andrew Ng's courses on DeepLearning.AI and Coursera (free to audit), or NPTEL machine learning courses from the IITs, with an optional certificate exam.
  4. Deep learning, hands-on: fast.ai, and Andrej Karpathy's "Neural Networks: Zero to Hero" series on YouTube.
  5. Build with AI models: learn to call an AI model from code using the OpenAI, Anthropic or Google developer documentation. Build one small app that solves a real problem.
  6. Show your work: publish projects on GitHub and try a Kaggle competition. A portfolio matters more than certificates.
