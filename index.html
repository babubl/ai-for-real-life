Your first win, in 15 minutes

Most people quit AI courses because they spend hours watching and never feel the "wow". So start the other way round: do one useful thing today. Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini on your phone (all free), start the timer and follow three steps.

15:00

  1. Explain something confusing (5 min). Take a photo of an electricity bill, a school circular, an insurance letter or a medicine strip, attach it, and paste: 
    Explain this to me like I'm 12. What is it, what are the important numbers or dates, and do I need to do anything?
  2. Talk to it (5 min). Tap the voice or microphone button and just speak: "I want to plan a weekend trip near my city for a family of four on a small budget. Ask me questions first." Answer out loud like a conversation.
  3. Make it yours (5 min). Pick a real problem from this week and paste: 
    Here's something I need to sort out this week: [describe it]. Ask me up to 5 questions, one at a time, then give me a simple step-by-step plan.
  4. Done? You just used vision, voice and planning, three of the most powerful AI skills. Now take the quiz above for your full path, or start with AI School, the basics everyone needs first.

"Should I buy a Udemy course?" An honest answer

To get started: no. The basics are free, and the best free material comes from the companies building AI and from universities. It's also more current: AI tools change every few months, and a course recorded last year is already teaching old screens. Watching hours of video also isn't the same as using AI. You learn by doing small real tasks, which is what this site is built around.

Free, and better for beginners

  • Elements of AI: University of Helsinki's gentle intro
  • Anthropic Academy and OpenAI Academy: straight from the makers
  • 3Blue1Brown: beautiful visual explanations
  • DeepLearning.AI: "AI for Everyone" and short courses, free to audit
  • This site's AI School and 12-week course: hands-on, in order

When paying is worth it

  • You need a recognised certificate for a job or promotion (from Google, Microsoft, AWS, or a university)
  • You want deep technical skills: Python, machine learning engineering, data science
  • You need a live cohort with a mentor who reviews your work and keeps you on deadline
  • You've finished the free basics and know exactly what gap you're filling

Red flags in any course

  • Promises of income ("earn ₹1 lakh a month with ChatGPT")
  • "Secret prompts" or "1,000-prompt packs"
  • Countdown timers and a "sale" that never ends
  • No date of last update, or older than 6 months for a tools course
  • All videos, no projects or exercises
  • An instructor with no real work you can look at

Before you buy anything: check the last-updated date, watch the free preview, read the 3-star reviews (they're the most honest), and check the refund policy.

AI safety for your family

AI makes scams more convincing: a cloned voice, a fake video of a celebrity, a perfect-looking investment app. Ten minutes on this section can save someone you love a lot of money. Share it with parents and grandparents first.

The "emergency" callA crying voice that sounds exactly like your child or relative, asking for money urgently. Voices can be cloned from a few seconds of audio. Agree on a family code word, hang up, and call back on the number you have saved.
Celebrity investment videosA famous face promoting a trading app or a "government AI scheme" with guaranteed returns. These are usually deepfakes. Real investments never guarantee high monthly returns.
"AI trading bots"Anyone promising fixed profits from an AI bot is selling a scam. Check that an adviser is registered with SEBI before trusting any advice.
What never to pasteOTPs, passwords, Aadhaar, PAN, card or bank details, other people's private information. Not in any chatbot, and not to any "AI agent" message.
Fake AI appsDownload only official apps, and check the publisher's name: OpenAI for ChatGPT, Anthropic for Claude, Google for Gemini. Look-alikes steal data or charge hidden fees.
Kids and AITalk about it openly. AI can be wrong, it isn't a friend, and you should never share photos or your address with it. Use it together for homework at first.
Check before you shareShocking photo or video on WhatsApp? Look for odd hands, blurry text, strange lighting, and search for it on a news site before forwarding.
Privacy settingsIn ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini settings, you can turn off training on your chats and review what's remembered about you. Take two minutes to check.
1930Lost money to a cyber fraud in India? Call the national cyber crime helpline 1930 immediately, or report at cybercrime.gov.in. The faster you report, the better the chance of freezing the money.
